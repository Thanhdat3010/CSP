Abstract. Camouflaged Object Detection (COD) remains challenging
due to the strong visual similarity between foreground objects and their
surrounding background, where conventional data augmentation tech-
niques often disrupt contextual consistency and limit performance gains.
In this work, we propose Camouflaged Semantic Partitioning (CSP), a
context-aware data augmentation framework tailored for COD. CSP is
based on semantic partitioning driven by heterogeneous feature ensem-
bles that integrate high-level semantic representations with appearance-
level cues to better capture object–background relationships. The fusion
combines scene-level semantic concepts extracted by pre-trained mod-
els with comprehensive visual descriptors computed using deterministic
mathematical functions. As a result, CSP enforces context-consistent
clustering to partition the dataset into semantically homogeneous sub-
sets, allowing augmentation strategies to be applied more effectively. Ex-
periments, which were conducted on standard camouflaged object detec-
tion benchmarks with no augmentation or augmentation without parti-
tioning, demonstrate that CSP consistently improves augmentation effec-
tiveness. Overall, CSP provides a principled framework for context-aware
augmentation and demonstrates the effectiveness of semantic partition-
ing for improving camouflage detection performance.
Keywords: Camouflaged Object Detection · Data Augmentation · Se-
mantic Partitioning.

Introduction
Camouflaged Object Detection (COD) aims to recognize and segment objects
seamlessly embedded into their surroundings [3,9]. While crucial for applica-
tions like wildlife monitoring and medical diagnoses [4], COD remains highly
challenging. The intrinsic visual and structural similarity between targets and

backgrounds continues to bottleneck model performance, even with recent ad-
vancements in deep learning architectures [6].
Although data augmentation is essential for training robust models, conven-
tional methods—such as Mosaic, Copy-Paste [5], Fourier Domain Adaptation
(FDA) [14], and Poisson Image Editing [13]—contradict the core mechanism of
camouflage. Blindly applying these techniques across an entire dataset mixes
disparate scenes, instantly destroying the camouflage illusion. Instead of synthe-
sizing challenging cases, they generate trivial targets with unnatural artifacts,
teaching the network to rely on structural noise rather than genuine camouflage
cues.
To address this limitation, we propose Camouflaged Semantic Partitioning
(CSP), a context-aware data augmentation framework tailored for COD. CSP
enforces environmental semantics by partitioning data into homogeneous sub-
sets, ensuring that augmentations only blend contextually compatible scenes.
To capture complex object-background relationships, CSP leverages a hetero-
geneous feature ensemble, integrating scene-level semantic concepts from foun-
dation models [12] with low-level appearance descriptors (color, texture, and
frequency).
Our framework employs a hyperparameter optimization strategy [1] to guide
a constrained hierarchical clustering pipeline [7]. A rigorous post-processing al-
gorithm further bounds cluster sizes while preserving semantic distributions,
establishing a reliable, context-consistent foundation for subsequent augmenta-
tions.
In summary, our main contributions are:
• We propose CSP, a context-aware augmentation framework that preserves
semantic integrity to significantly improve COD performance.
• We design a heterogeneous feature fusion approach, optimized via an auto-
mated search [1], seamlessly integrating scene-level semantics with appearance-
level cues.
• We introduce a scalable clustering and post-processing pipeline [7] that
strictly bounds cluster sizes for practical data augmentation.
• Extensive experiments on the CAMO-FS dataset [11] demonstrate that CSP
consistently boosts performance across cutting-edge architectures, including
YOLOv8n (CNN) [6] and RT-DETR-L (Transformer) [15].

Related Work
The field of COD focuses on identifying and segmenting objects that deliber-
ately conceal their textures to blend into their surroundings [3,9]. Pioneering
works established foundational architectures, such as the Anabranch Network
[9] and SINet [3], to tackle the high intrinsic similarities between targets and
backgrounds. Subsequent research addressed data scarcity through specialized
datasets like CAMO-FS and few-shot learning frameworks [11]. Concurrently,
the adaptation of state-of-the-art generic object detectors, such as the YOLO
series [6], has pushed performance boundaries. However, these advancements

remain predominantly architecture- or loss-centric, focusing on complex atten-
tion modules to untangle visual ambiguities. They uniformly treat the underly-
ing training dataset as a static entity, leaving the potential of data-level opti-
mization underexplored. In contrast, our proposed CSP framework introduces a
purely data-centric perspective, restructuring the training data into semantically
homogeneous clusters to unlock the feature-extraction capabilities of powerful
backbones without adding inference overhead.
While data augmentation is essential for expanding training diversity, con-
ventional techniques present critical limitations when applied to COD. Stan-
dard blending and spatial methods, such as the Simple Copy-Paste strategy [5],
or even frequency-domain adaptations like CamoFA [8], improve model robust-
ness in generic tasks but intuitively contradict the camouflage mechanism when
applied globally. By randomly pasting instance masks or mixing frequency com-
ponents across mismatched environments, these methods disregard the delicate
contextual alignment between the object and its background. This synthesizes
unrealistic artifacts rather than challenging camouflage cases, teaching the net-
work to rely on structural noise [8]. In stark contrast, CSP fundamentally pre-
serves the camouflage illusion by ensuring that data mixing is strictly confined
within context-consistent subgroups, allowing models to exploit augmentation
variance without synthesizing context-disrupted training samples.
To achieve this context-aware partitioning, our framework synergistically
leverages advancements in feature representation and unsupervised clustering.
Foundation models like DINOv2 [12] provide highly generalized semantics, while
algorithms like Ward’s hierarchical clustering [7] and the Caliński-Harabasz (CH)
index [2] offer mathematically sound mechanisms to group high-dimensional
data. Integrating these into a multi-stage pipeline requires extensive tuning,
a challenge effectively addressed by optimization frameworks like Optuna [1].
While these tools are typically utilized in isolation, CSP integrates them into a
highly customized preprocessing engine. We reframe feature fusion as an auto-
mated optimization problem, dynamically searching for optimal feature weights
by maximizing the CH score within the semantic subspace. Coupled with a rig-
orous post-processing step to strictly bound cluster sizes (Tmin and Tmax), this
orchestration transforms generic mathematical tools into a scalable, context-
aware partitioning framework tailored explicitly for camouflage scenes.

Once the mathematical optimum is reached and the final fused vector (VFINAL)
is formulated, the system executes a definitive Ward clustering operation. Since
Ward’s method minimizes intra-cluster variance without restricting cardinality,
this raw clustering naturally yields imbalanced sub-groups ranging from massive
to exceptionally tiny. To ensure these subsets are viable for stable deep learning

ular sub-clusters, while those falling below the minimum threshold (Tmin) are
merged with medium clusters exhibiting the closest intra-cluster variance to pool
sufficient samples. The culmination of this process yields uniformly bounded,
context-aware sub-datasets perfectly primed for advanced data augmentation.
3.2
Optuna and Ward
To effectively partition the heterogeneous feature space into semantically homo-
geneous subsets, we employ Ward’s hierarchical clustering [7]. Unlike centroid-
based algorithms such as K-Means, which require a predefined number of clus-
ters and struggle with the highly ambiguous boundaries inherent to camouflage,
Ward’s method agglomeratively merges data points by minimizing the total
intra-cluster variance. This objective function inherently favors the formation of

5
compact, closely-knit clusters, successfully grouping images that share identical
structural and semantic environments. More importantly, Ward’s method yields
a deterministic hierarchical dendrogram. This structural transparency is critical
for our framework, as it allows us to apply an algorithmic post-processing step to
rigidly bound cluster sizes between Tmin and Tmax. This guarantees that every
subset contains sufficient samples to support variance during data augmentation,
without becoming so broad that contextual consistency is compromised.
However, the efficacy of Ward’s clustering is entirely dependent on the spa-
tial balance of the underlying multi-modal feature vectors. Because high-level
semantics (DINOv2) and low-level appearance descriptors (FFT, LBP) possess
fundamentally different dimensionalities and geometric significance, manually
deriving the optimal weighting configuration is mathematically intractable.
To resolve this bottleneck, we integrate Optuna [1], a state-of-the-art hyper-
parameter optimization framework driven by Bayesian optimization (specifically,
the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator). Optuna was explicitly selected over grid
or random search methodologies due to its define-by-run design and exceptional
efficiency in navigating highly constrained, continuous search spaces. Within the
CSP pipeline, Optuna acts as the automated orchestration engine, dynamically
searching for the precise feature-fusion weights by maximizing the semantic CH
score [2]. By leveraging Optuna, we systematically eliminate human guesswork,
allowing the framework to mathematically deduce the ideal inductive bias re-
quired to untangle complex camouflage environments.

3.4
Balancing Process
Ward’s clustering minimizes global intra-cluster variance but ignores cluster
cardinality, naturally yielding a severe long-tail distribution of massive super-
clusters and fragmented micro-clusters. To stabilize batch variance for neural
network training, we implement a deterministic two-phase algorithm that bounds
cluster capacities within [Tmin, Tmax] while preserving original semantic bound-
aries.
Oversized clusters (|C| > Tmax) are recursively partitioned. We compute the
theoretical minimum number of required sub-clusters, kmin = ⌈|C|/Tmax⌉, and
perform a localized grid search for k ∈[kmin, kmin+2]. The split maximizing the
CH index is selected, guaranteeing that oversized groups are divided precisely
along their internal semantic fault lines.
In addition, fragmented micro-clusters (|C| < Tmin) are consolidated using
Ward’s minimum variance criterion to protect existing semantic purity. For a

Experimental Results
4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets. We extensively evaluate our proposed framework on the CAMO-FS
dataset [11]. This dataset is specifically designed for evaluating camouflaged
instances in highly complex natural scenes, containing diverse animal categories
heavily entangled with their surrounding habitats. The dataset is inherently
challenging due to its extreme visual ambiguities, making it an ideal benchmark
for assessing data augmentation effectiveness. In our experiments, the dataset is
meticulously partitioned into a training set of 2,424 images and a testing set of
428 images.
Evaluation Metrics. Following standard object detection protocols, we
adopt the widely used Average Precision (AP) metrics established by the MS
COCO benchmark [10]. Specifically, we report the overall mean Average Pre-
cision across multiple Intersection over Union (IoU) thresholds (mAP50−95), as
well as AP at a strict IoU threshold of 0.50 (mAP50).
4.2
Baseline and Augmentation Strategies
To rigorously validate the robustness of our framework across different augmen-
tation paradigms, we evaluate four distinct strategies: FDA for frequency ma-
nipulation, Mosaic for spatial structural mixing, Copy-Paste for instance-level
semantics, and Poisson blending for seamless gradient mixing. For each strategy,
we compare three configurations:
• Baseline: The model is trained strictly on the original dataset without ap-
plying any spatial or pixel-level mixing.
• Global Augmentation: The augmentation techniques are applied conven-
tionally by randomly selecting paired images from the entire, unpartitioned
dataset.

4.4
Results
The comprehensive quantitative results comparing the baseline, global augmen-
tations, and our CSP-guided augmentations across two distinct architectures—YOLOv8n
(CNN-based) and RT-DETR-L (Transformer-based)—are presented in Table 2.
Three main observations can be drawn from these multi-architecture results.
First, for the CNN-based YOLOv8n, naively applying global augmentations
tends to severely hurt strict localization metrics (mAP50−95). Global Mosaic
and Global Poisson reduce mAP50−95 from the baseline value of 23.09% down
to 21.73% and 21.59%, respectively. By disrupting the spatial layout through
random patches or blending boundaries across semantically mismatched envi-
ronments, unconstrained mixing encourages the local receptive fields of CNNs
to rely on unrealistic structural artifacts rather than genuine camouflage cues.
This degradation empirically supports our core premise: mixing visually incom-
patible scenes actively breaks the contextual entanglement that COD critically
depends on.
Second, the integration of the high-capacity RT-DETR-L architecture re-
veals the distinct regularization requirements of Vision Transformers. Unlike

YOLOv8n, RT-DETR-L achieves massive performance leaps even with naive
Global augmentations—jumping from a baseline mAP50 of 41.45% to 53.56%
under Global Mosaic. Because Transformers lack strong translation equivariance
and are notoriously data-hungry, they overfit heavily on the limited CAMO-FS
baseline (2,424 images). Consequently, unconstrained global mixing acts as an
aggressive regularizer. It expands the data distribution sufficiently to prevent
overfitting, yielding a large initial performance spike despite the introduction of
contextual noise.
Finally, regardless of the underlying architecture’s inherent capacity or induc-
tive bias, our context-aware CSP framework consistently establishes the high-
est performance ceiling. While global augmentations provide the raw data vol-
ume necessary for Transformer regularization, they fundamentally ignore object-
background semantic relationships. By constraining image sampling to semanti-
cally coherent sub-groups, CSP ensures that the synthesized data provides not
only variance but also high-fidelity contextual consistency. For YOLOv8n, CSP
Poisson attains the peak mAP50−95 of 24.60%. More impressively, for RT-DETR-
L, CSP consistently outperforms its Global counterparts across all four manip-
ulation strategies, with CSP Mosaic reaching an outstanding mAP50 of 54.97%
and mAP50−95 of 42.60%. These consistent improvements robustly demonstrate
that CSP is architecture-agnostic: it prevents low-capacity models from mem-
orizing unnatural noise, while simultaneously supplying high-capacity models
with the contextually accurate variance required to master complex camouflage
environments.

Limitations
Despite the substantial performance improvements achieved by the proposed
CSP framework, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, our pipeline
introduces a non-trivial computational overhead during the offline data prepa-
ration phase. The extraction of heterogeneous features—particularly relying on
massive foundation models like DINOv2 and high-dimensional transformations
like FFT—coupled with the Optuna-guided hierarchical clustering, requires ded-
icated computational resources and processing time prior to the actual neural
network training.
Second, the current framework relies on empirically established hard con-
straints (Tmin and Tmax) to bound cluster sizes and maintain optimal batch
formations. While these thresholds effectively prevent the emergence of degener-
ate clusters within the CAMO-FS dataset, applying CSP to distinctly different
domains—such as medical polyp segmentation, industrial defect detection, or
aerial thermal camouflage—would necessitate domain-specific hyperparameter
recalibrations.
Finally, natural camouflage datasets inherently exhibit a severe long-tail dis-
tribution. Forcing extremely rare categories to satisfy the Tmin requirement
might occasionally group them with sub-optimal, marginally related backgrounds.
This phenomenon can slightly dilute the semantic purity of those specific minor-

Conclusion
We introduced CSP, a data-centric framework tailored for COD tasks. By recog-
nizing that conventional, global augmentation strategies inadvertently destroy
the crucial object-background entanglement—thereby generating unrealistic ar-
tifacts and hindering model performance—CSP enforces strict semantic bound-
aries for synthetic data generation.
Our approach leverages a highly optimized, heterogeneous feature fusion
pipeline that seamlessly integrates high-level foundational semantics with low-
level appearance and frequency cues. This formulation ensures that advanced
augmentation techniques are applied exclusively within contextually homoge-
neous subsets. Extensive experiments on the challenging CAMO-FS dataset
demonstrate the superiority of our data-centric paradigm. Without adding a sin-
gle parameter or computational burden to the inference phase, the integration
of CSP successfully mitigated the degradation caused by unconstrained mixing
and yielded significant improvements in strict localization metrics across both
CNN-based (YOLOv8n) and Transformer-based (RT-DETR-L) architectures.
Ultimately, CSP provides a mathematically grounded, principled methodol-
ogy for synthetic data generation in camouflage environments. Future research
will focus on developing dynamic, adaptive cluster-bounding algorithms to better
accommodate extreme long-tail distributions, and extending this context-aware
framework to other dense-prediction vision tasks where environmental integrity
is paramount.
